# Your first lesson

This walks through making a short lesson from start to finish: a title, a
section with a passage, a spelling list, a question and a picture, then a look
at the result and a printout.

You don't need an account for any of this. Everything you do is saved in your
browser as you go. You only need to sign in if you want to publish the lesson
to the hub or save it to the cloud.

## 1. Open the editor

On the home page, press **Open the editor**. You can also press **New
lesson** in the app header at any time to start a fresh lesson.

The first time you open the editor, a small **Create your first lesson** guide
appears in the corner. It sums up the same steps as this page, and you can
work in the editor while it's open. Close it whenever you like, and bring it
back with the **?** button (**How to create a lesson**) at the top of the
editor.

## 2. Give the lesson a title

At the top of the editor, under **Document title**, click the title and type
your own, for example "Domestic Cats". You can change it at any time.

Under the title you can pick an age range (it starts as **Any age**), which
prints on the lesson. If you'd like ideas for a topic, **Suggest ideas** offers
some for that age range (see [AI writing help](./ai-writing-help.md)).

## 3. Add a section

Lessons are made of sections. Press **Add section** (or the round **+** button
in the bottom-right corner), type a name under **Section name**, such as
"Ancient Egypt", and press **Add**.

The new section has a row of buttons for adding content: **Add text**, **Add
image**, **Search images**, **Add question**, **Spelling words**, **VAKT
activity** and **Generate with AI**. The row sits under whichever block you're
working on, or at the bottom of the section.

## 4. Write the passage

Press **Add text** and type the passage into the box. Write the words you want
spelled in ALL CAPS:

> Cats were first tamed in ANCIENT EGYPT, where people kept them to protect
> grain from MICE.

You can make words bold, italic or underlined, and add footnotes, from the
small toolbar on the text block (see
[Formatting, footnotes & sources](./formatting-and-footnotes.md)).

## 5. Fill in the spelling list

Press **Spelling words** to add a spelling list. Then press the wand button on
the list (**Fill in every ALL-CAPS word from the lesson text**), and every word
you wrote in capitals is put in the list for you. You can also type words in
yourself, use **Add word** for more rows, and remove any you don't want.

## 6. Ask a question

Press **Add question** and pick a type. Each type has a color and a short
description in the menu; for this one, pick **Single answer**. Type the
question where it says "Type the question..." and its answer under **Answer**:

> Where were cats first tamed? **Answer:** EGYPT

The eight types and what they're for are explained in
[Question blocks](./question-blocks.md).

## 7. Add a picture

Press **Search images**, type something like "cat", press **Search**, and
click a picture to add it. Its credit is filled in for you; add a caption
saying what it shows under **Caption (optional)**. To use a picture from your
own device instead, press **Add image**. See [Pictures](./images.md) for more.

Every block has buttons to move it up or down or delete it, and with a mouse
you can drag a block by its handle.

## 8. Preview it

Press **Preview** at the top of the editor to see the lesson exactly as a
reader will. Press **Back to editing** to carry on working.

If you'd like a second opinion, **Check** looks the lesson over and lists
anything that needs attention (see [Lesson checks](./lesson-checks.md)).

## 9. Print or download it

Open the **Export** menu at the top of the editor:

- **Export DOCX** downloads a Word document.
- **Print PDF** downloads a PDF, ready to print.
- **Save to Google Docs** puts it in your Google Drive, where available.

On a phone, these are in the **Lesson actions** menu (the three dots). See
[Exporting & printing](./exporting.md) for what the printed lesson looks like.

## 10. Publish it (optional)

To share the lesson with others, open **Save to cloud** and choose **Publish
to hub**, or **Save as draft** to keep a private copy in the cloud that only
you can see. If you aren't signed in yet, you'll be taken to the sign-in page
first; signing in is usually by a link sent to your email, with no password
(see
[Lesson hub & accounts](./hub-and-accounts.md)).

## Where your lesson lives

Your lesson stays in this browser, saved automatically, and every version is
kept in its history. All your lessons are listed under **On this device** (see
[Lessons on this device](./local-lessons.md)). Until you save a lesson to the
cloud, it's only on this device, so clearing your browsing data would remove
it.

From here, the [overview](./overview.md) lists everything else the editor can
do.
